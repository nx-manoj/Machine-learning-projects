What to do with it

Four things this changes about how we talk about air

01

Never quote a national average without the panel. The network grew twelve-fold; an unqualified average measures coverage, not air.

02

Compare within a season. A November-to-November comparison is the only one that separates policy from meteorology, given a 2.6x annual cycle.

03

Time outdoor activity to the afternoon trough. The 17:00 minimum is 41% below the 22:00 peak — larger than most interventions achieve in a year.

04

Spend the next effort on data, not models. Meteorology and fire counts are worth more than any model change tested here.

Recommendations follow from the panel construction, the seasonal amplitude and the forecast ceiling

Limits

What seven years of AQI data cannot tell you

What this cannot tell you
  • Which pollutant. AQI reports the worst sub-index, so it hides what drove a day; this dataset has no pollutant breakdown.
  • Who is exposed. One number per city per day erases the intra-urban variation that determines personal exposure.
  • Whether a city really improved. A relocated or recalibrated monitor produces exactly the signature Varanasi's 65% fall shows.
  • What happens after 2020. That year is a structural break; models trained across it learn a level that never recurs.
What would make it usable
  • Join PM2.5, PM10 and NO2 to say what is driving the index.
  • Add meteorology and satellite fire counts — the features most likely to beat persistence by more than 9%.
  • Model at station level, hierarchically, rather than collapsing to city medians.
  • Test a 3-day horizon, where persistence decays and a real model earns its keep.

The ceiling on forecasting is the dataset, not the algorithm. Every model tested here has no information the previous month did not already contain — which is exactly why the simplest model wins.

Limitations are properties of the dataset, not of the modelling approach
